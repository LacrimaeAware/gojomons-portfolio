Choices, builds
and the fights between.

Some decisions happen on the road. Others happen while making the game. These examples connect an implemented rule to the choice—or experiment—behind it.

Along the road

One more route?

One creature is barely standing. Another has fainted. The next road offers a larger reward and stronger opposition. The party’s condition changes what that reward is worth.

Prepare for the whole route

Cinder Vow spends 15% of a creature’s current HP. It raises Attack and Special Attack by one stage at the start of each fight on the next route. A healing supply protects the team now; a vow invests in the fights ahead.

Shape the next fight

A campfire offers party recovery or a level for one living creature. A new move changes its automatic choices. With Hushreed, you can also remove a move and focus its kit.

The tuning process

Three ways to
change the pace.

Global damage, fatigue and shield decay affect different parts of a fight. These July experiments show the differences across 800 six-creature matchups per setting.

Higher damage moves the whole fight.

At 1.5× damage, the median falls from 22 to 16 rounds. The change also reverses the winner in 181 of 800 matchups: faster combat changes which builds succeed.

A separate check:
single-type stats

A later experiment tested seven stat multipliers for single-type creatures against dual-type opponents. Training selected the existing stats. On 1,024 fresh, side-swapped scenarios, the single-type group scored 49.7%, with a 95% scenario-bootstrap interval of 47.2–52.3%.

A tool for the work

Go straight to
the encounter.

The Playtest Launcher lets me choose an attacker, move and target, or open a specific route state in the actual game. I can inspect an animation or interaction without replaying a run to reach it.

It uses the game’s scene and combat systems. That makes it useful for checking whether a rule, its visual effect and its sound agree.
