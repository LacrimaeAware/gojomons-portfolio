Combat pacing · July 27, 2026

The archived experiment varies global damage, fatigue and shield decay. Damage was tested at 1.0, 1.25, 1.5 and 2.0 with teams of one, three and six creatures; three- and six-creature teams use two active slots. Shield decay was tested at 0, 0.25, 0.33 and 0.5 with three- and six-creature teams. A four-condition six-creature experiment crosses damage 1.0/1.5 with fatigue off/on. All creatures are level 30.

Each condition has 3,000 one-creature, 1,200 three-creature or 800 six-creature battles, as applicable. Equipment, two common relics, combat effects and sustain cooldowns are part of this historical setup. Damage and fatigue comparisons use shield decay zero; shield comparisons use damage 1.0 and fatigue on. Some controls are the same recorded battles reused across studies.

What stays matched

Within each comparison, scenario IDs, seeds, species, order, format, level and relic lists match. The archived producer uses separate seeded generators for roster and loadout construction. It explicitly overwrites the constructor’s random style with the species’ authored primary style before serialization. Stats and move initialization follow deterministic construction paths.

Held-item identities and complete runtime snapshots were not saved in these CSVs. Their matching rests on the archived producer code. The engine’s effects loader could fall back silently, and these historical runs did not save an effects-activation manifest. The public bundle reproduces the recorded analysis; it contains the input records rather than an executable historical game build.

What the chart measures

For rounds below 120, the curve is the fraction of recorded battles whose duration exceeds that round. The endpoint at 120 is the fraction flagged unresolved at the cap. A fight resolved exactly at round 120 contributes to the duration distribution but not that unresolved count. The range control offers rounds 0–60, rounds 60–120 and the whole recorded fight. The tail uses a 0–4% scale for fatigue and 0–10% for damage or shields; the percentage ticks identify the scale.

Means include the capped duration of 120. Medians and counts summarize the complete recorded batch. Paired winner reversals count player-to-enemy and enemy-to-player wins; transitions involving draws or timeouts are recorded separately. No confidence interval is inferred from these historical batches.

At damage 1.5, 666 of the 800 six-creature fights became shorter than at 1.0, 30 stayed the same length, and 104 became longer. There were 181 decisive winner reversals. These details explain why a global pacing adjustment is also a change to the competitive relationships among builds.

Reproduce the analysis

Download and extract the pacing bundle. It contains the raw records, source hashes, analysis and expected outputs. Python 3.10 or later and its standard library are sufficient. From the extracted folder:

python analyze_pacing.py --data-root data --output derived

The command creates pacing-results.json and pacing-summary.csv. The JSON includes turn histograms, paired changes and all 24 condition summaries. The analysis validates retained scenario matching before calculating comparisons. The included hash manifest records the bundled inputs.

How this relates to the current game

On September 11, 2026, the development configuration retains damage 1.5 and shield decay 0.25. Fatigue still targets long fights, and now includes a 1.35 growth factor that was absent from the July configuration. The July measurements document the tuning process; current rules and content have continued to change.

Single-type stat study · September 2026

This separate experiment asks whether a blanket stat adjustment brings a single-type cohort’s average outcome against a dual-type cohort closer to 50%. It uses 41 single-type and 61 dual-type species at level 30, excluding legendary-tagged species and those with a further evolution condition. Roster seed 741911 fixes one style realization per species; the exported roster preserves the realized inputs.

For each scenario, sample one species from each cohort and a battle seed, then run both side orientations. A win scores 1, a loss 0, and a draw or 80-round timeout 0.5. Average the two orientations to obtain the scenario score. Family and subtype effects are active; held items, relics, master bonuses and weather are absent.

Seven multipliers from 0.80 to 1.40 share the same 256 training scenarios. The multiplier scales effective HP, Attack, Defense, Special Attack, Special Defense and Speed, with integer rounding. The current constructor already includes a 1.07 allowance for the single-type cohort, so an experimental multiplier of 1.00 preserves it.

Choose the multiplier closest to a 0.5 training score, breaking ties in ascending multiplier order. Training selected 1.00. The independent holdout uses 1,024 new scenarios and yields a mean score of 0.49707. Across its 2,048 battles there were 34 draws and no timeouts. Training and holdout together contain 5,632 battles.

The 95% bootstrap interval, 47.2–52.3%, resamples whole scenarios 4,000 times, preserving the two side orientations. A conservative Hoeffding interval is 45.5–54.0%. These intervals describe matchup and battle-seed sampling within this fixed roster. The holdout touches 829 of the 2,501 possible species pairs; 658 pairs appear once, so the displayed result is a cohort comparison.

Recorded checks include seed repeatability and 112 battles replayed in reverse parameter order in a fresh process. Their outcomes and turn counts matched. The public outcomes also include the roster and experimental settings.

Implemented choices

Shield decay removes the configured fraction of the remaining shield with integer rounding and a minimum one-point decrement. The plain 25% label identifies the tuning parameter. Fatigue applies escalating HP damage to both sides’ active creatures as a fight runs long.
